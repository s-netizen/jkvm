What we stand for

Four pillars of our mission

Everything JKVM does traces back to these commitments — to our past, our people, and our nation.

01

Cultural Preservation

Safeguarding Kashmiri Pandit traditions, language, and heritage for generations to come.

02

Intellectual Dialogue

Hosting discourse, seminars and open conversation on culture, history and civic life.

03

Leadership Building

Equipping young Kashmiri Pandits with the skills and platforms to lead with confidence.

04

National Integration

Strengthening the bond between the Kashmiri Pandit diaspora and the nation at large.

Our story

Born of displacement. Built on resolve.

JKVM came into existence in 1995, in the aftermath of the forced exodus of Kashmiri Hindus from the Valley — a community scattered but never severed from its roots.

"What we could not carry in our hands, we carried in our memory — and JKVM became the place to keep it alive."

What began as a small circle of concerned voices grew into a structured socio-cultural forum — one dedicated to documenting oral histories, organizing cultural festivals, and creating spaces where displaced families could reconnect with their identity even while rebuilding their lives across the country.

Over three decades, JKVM has widened its focus from preservation alone to active nation-building: mentoring young Kashmiri Pandits, running civic dialogue programs, and forging ties between the community and the wider national fabric — always rooted in the belief that memory and progress are not opposites.
